Element 1: The Client Relationship

8 Questions · Verb levels: Understand, Apply, Analyze

Builds on baseline exams (CIRE, Retail/Institutional Securities Exam). Applies KYP, KYC, suitability, and account appropriateness rules specifically to derivatives.

1.1 Product Due Diligence

Both the Investment Dealer (firm) AND the Approved Person (individual rep) must research and understand a product before recommending or selling it.

  • Must distinguish between: Options (right, not obligation), Futures (obligation for both parties), and Swaps (exchange of cash flows).

1.2 Know-Your-Product (KYP) — Five Dimensions

DimensionWhat to assess
StructureHow the product is built — listed on exchange or OTC? How does it pay out?
FeaturesWhat rights or obligations does it create? What is the underlying asset?
RisksMarket risk, leverage risk, liquidity risk, counterparty risk.
Initial & Ongoing CostsPremium, margin requirements, roll costs, management fees.
Impact of CostsHow costs affect actual return — high costs can turn a winning trade into a loss.

1.3 Account Appropriateness

The firm must confirm the account TYPE is appropriate for derivatives trading. Cash accounts suit some option strategies; margin accounts are required for short options and futures. This is separate from suitability.

1.4 Know-Your-Client (KYC)

Five suitability factors: Investment objectives · Risk tolerance · Derivatives knowledge and experience · Financial situation · Time horizon.

Client TypeDescription
Retail ClientIndividual investor. Full KYC and suitability obligations apply on every recommendation.
Institutional ClientBanks, pension funds, corporations. Reduced KYC — deemed sophisticated. No trade-specific suitability required.
HedgerClient using derivatives to offset an existing business risk. May qualify as institutional under specific CIRO procedures.
⚠️ Exam trap: Institutional clients get fewer protections, but hedgers are NOT automatically institutional — there is a specific qualification process.

1.5 Suitability

Each trade must be suitable for the specific client — assessed against objectives, risk tolerance, knowledge, financial situation, and time horizon. Retail clients require full assessment on every recommendation.

1.6 Internal Escalation and Delegation

If a client wants to trade a derivative the rep is not qualified for, the matter must be escalated to an appropriately registered person. Some delegation is permitted; some is prohibited (e.g., to unlicensed persons).

1.7 Communicating with Clients

  • All communications must be fair, balanced, and not misleading.
  • Social media, emails, and videos are all subject to CIRO rules.
  • Off-channel communications (personal WhatsApp, personal email) are prohibited or heavily restricted.

1.8 Client Record Requirements

KYC information must be kept current. All trades, instructions, and communications must be documented and retained per CIRO retention rules. When a material change occurs (e.g., job change, significant income increase), update the profile and confirm with the client before the next transaction.

Element 2: Regulatory Documentation

10 Questions · Verb levels: Understand, Apply

2.1 Client Interaction Requirements

  • Firms must have documented policies and procedures for soliciting clients and running their derivatives business.
  • Specific procedures must confirm whether a client qualifies as a hedger.
  • A qualified hedger may be reclassified as institutional — changing the level of regulatory protection.
  • Clients must receive reports before derivatives on their account expire.

2.2 Reporting Requirements

ReportPurpose
All Positions ReportReport all open positions — covered (hedged) and uncovered (naked/speculative).
Concentration ReportFlag when a client or firm holds an unusually large position in a single derivative.
P&L StatementsProfit and loss statements for active derivatives accounts.
Exception ReportsFlag unusual activity or rule exceptions for supervisory review.
Monthly Net Position ReportThe firm's overall net derivatives position each month.
Capital RequirementsMinimum capital must be maintained against open positions. Position limits cap how large positions can get.
Concentration capital: When a position is unusually large relative to the market or position limits, the firm must hold additional concentration capital as a buffer.

2.3 Prohibited Trading Practices

  • Trading using prohibited underlying interests
  • Trading without adequate margin
  • Trading beyond margin and credit limits
  • Cumulative losses exceeding risk limits
  • Delivery month trading restrictions
  • Excessive risk to account guarantors
  • Insider trading — trading on material non-public information

2.5 Account Opening Documents

DocumentPurpose / Key Rule
Derivatives Account Application FormMust be completed before the initial trade. No grace period.
Derivatives Trading AgreementContract governing how trades are handled between firm and client.
Derivatives Risk Disclosure StatementCIRO-approved. Must be provided before trading begins. Client must acknowledge receipt.
OEO OTC Retail DisclosureFor OEO accounts offering OTC derivatives to retail clients. Must disclose % of accounts profitable for each of the last four quarters.
Margin Agreement FormAuthorizes the firm to collect margin and sets margin terms.
Futures Porting DisclosuresRequired when futures positions are transferred between dealers.
Hedge AgreementDocuments the client's hedging relationship and intent. Affects margin treatment (hedge margin rates).
Authority to Transfer Funds FormAuthorizes movement of funds within or between accounts.
⚠️ Know WHICH document is required for WHICH situation. The OEO profitability disclosure is frequently tested.

2.6 Client Statements

StatementContent
Monthly Account StatementsFull summary including all open positions and transactions. For options on futures: must show type of option, strike price, and delivery date.
Trade ConfirmationsSent after each trade — confirms all trade details.
Statement of Purchase and SaleIssued on closing trades — shows the realized profit or loss. For commodity futures: must show the futures exchange, delivery month, and net profit or loss.

2.7 NI 93-101: Derivatives Business Conduct

  • Fair dealing with all derivatives parties.
  • Client assets must be disclosed, segregated, and protected.
  • Firms must have policies/procedures, a Senior Derivatives Manager, and internal reporting obligations.
  • The Quebec Derivatives Act applies for derivatives activity in Quebec.

Element 3: Types and Features of Derivatives

22 Questions · Verb levels: Understand, Analyze

One of the two highest-weighted elements (22 questions each with Element 4). A derivative is a financial contract whose value is derived from an underlying asset — you own the contract, not the underlying.

3.1 Futures and Forwards

Standardized, legally binding agreement to buy or sell a specific quantity of an asset at a predetermined price on a future date. BOTH parties are obligated — unlike options, there is no choice at expiry.

TermDefinition
Long PositionThe buyer — profits if price goes UP.
Short PositionThe seller — profits if price goes DOWN.
Listed (Exchange-traded)Standardized contracts on an exchange. Exchange/clearinghouse acts as guarantor. Lower counterparty risk.
OTC (Over-the-Counter)Custom contracts between two parties. More flexible but higher counterparty risk.
Cash SettlementPrice difference paid in cash at expiry — no physical delivery.
Physical SettlementActual delivery of the underlying asset (common in commodity futures).
Mark-to-MarketPositions are repriced DAILY — gains and losses credited/debited each day.
Margin ObligationBoth buyers and sellers must post margin — a performance deposit (NOT a loan).

3.1A Contango and Backwardation

TermDefinition
ContangoFutures prices HIGHER than current spot. Far-dated contracts more expensive. Normal when storage costs are significant. Curve slopes upward.
BackwardationFutures prices LOWER than current spot. Near-term contracts more expensive. Common when there is an immediate supply shortage. Curve slopes downward.
Calendar spread strategy: In contango, buy near-term + sell far-term to profit if contango narrows.

3.1B Bond Futures and Duration Hedging

Bond futures are used to manage interest rate (duration) risk. When rates rise, bond prices fall — managers SHORT bond futures to hedge.

Contracts to Hedge = (Portfolio Value × Portfolio Duration) ÷ (Futures Duration × Futures Notional)

Example: $10M portfolio, duration 8.4 yrs; Futures: duration 7.2 yrs, notional $100,000
= ($10M × 8.4) / (7.2 × $100,000) = 117 contracts SHORT
Fig 1 — Long Futures: profit when price rises above entry
Fig 2 — Short Futures: profit when price falls below entry

3.2 Options

An option gives the BUYER the RIGHT (but not the obligation) to buy or sell at the strike price. The SELLER (writer) must fulfill if the buyer exercises.

TermDefinition
Call OptionRight to BUY at the strike. Buyer profits if price rises above strike.
Put OptionRight to SELL at the strike. Buyer profits if price falls below strike.
Long (Buyer)Pays premium. Has right, no obligation. Max loss = premium paid.
Short (Writer)Receives premium. Has OBLIGATION. Potentially large/unlimited losses.
American StyleCan be exercised at ANY TIME up to and including expiry.
European StyleCan ONLY be exercised ON the expiry date.
At-the-Money (ATM)Strike = current price. Intrinsic value = 0. Maximum time value.
In-the-Money (ITM)Call: Spot > Strike. Put: Spot < Strike. Has positive intrinsic value.
Out-of-the-Money (OTM)Call: Spot < Strike. Put: Spot > Strike. Intrinsic value = 0.
Fig 3 — Long Call: unlimited upside, max loss = premium
Fig 4 — Short Call: max profit = premium, unlimited loss
Fig 5 — Long Put: profit rises as price falls below breakeven
Fig 6 — Short Put: max profit = premium, loss if price falls

Exotic Options

TypeDescription
BinaryAll-or-nothing payout. Fixed amount if condition met, zero otherwise.
BermudanExercise on specific pre-determined dates (between American and European).
Barrier (Knock-in/out)Activates (knock-in) or deactivates (knock-out) when underlying hits a barrier level.
AsianPayout based on AVERAGE price over a period, not just the price at expiry. Cheaper than standard options.
Prediction/ForecastingPayout depends on whether a specific event occurs (e.g., election outcome).
Fig 7 — CFD Payoff: mirrors the underlying price move, amplified by leverage

3.4 Contracts for Difference (CFDs)

Agreement to exchange the difference in value of an asset from open to close. Never take delivery of the underlying. No fixed expiry. Highly leveraged. OTC only for retail clients in Canada.

3.5 Swaps

Swap TypeDescription
Interest Rate SwapExchange fixed interest payments for floating (or vice versa). Most common type. Notional principal is NOT exchanged.
Currency SwapExchange interest and principal in one currency for another. Manages FX risk. Notional IS exchanged.
Equity SwapExchange total return of equity/index for a fixed or floating rate. Gain equity exposure without owning shares.
Commodity SwapExchange fixed commodity price for floating market price. Lock in prices for producers/consumers.
Credit Default Swap (CDS)Protection buyer pays premiums; protection seller pays out if a credit event (e.g., default) occurs. Like credit insurance.
Variance SwapPayout based on realized variance vs fixed variance. Used to trade volatility directly without delta hedging.
Fig 8 — Interest Rate Swap: Party A pays fixed, receives floating; notional NOT exchanged
Fig 9 — Credit Default Swap: protection buyer pays premiums; seller pays out on credit event

3.8 Leverage

Leverage Ratio = Notional Contract Value ÷ Capital Required (margin or premium)

Example: $100,000 futures contract, $5,000 margin → 20:1 leverage

3.9 Structured Products

TypeStructure
Principal-Protected Note (PPN)Zero-coupon bond (matures to return principal) + Long call option (provides upside participation). Participation rate = how much of the gain you receive.
Capital at Risk ProductHigher return potential but principal is not protected. Often uses knock-in puts — protection only activates if underlying falls below a barrier.

Element 4: Derivative Pricing

22 Questions · Verb levels: Understand, Apply (includes calculations)

4.2 Futures Fair Value — Cost of Carry

Fair Value (Future) = Spot Price + Cost of Carry
Cost of Carry = Financing Cost + Storage + Insurance − Income Received (e.g., dividends)
Basis = Spot Price − Futures Price (converges to 0 at expiry)
TermDefinition
ConvergenceFutures price → Spot price as expiry nears. Basis → 0 at expiry.
Cash & Carry ArbitrageFutures OVERPRICED → buy spot + sell futures = risk-free profit.
Reverse Cash & CarryFutures UNDERPRICED → sell spot + buy futures = risk-free profit.

4.2C Interest Rate Parity — Forward Exchange Rates

Forward Exchange Rate = Spot × (1 + r_domestic) / (1 + r_foreign)

Example: Spot = 1.3500 CAD/USD, r_Canada = 3%, r_US = 5%
F = 1.3500 × (1.03 / 1.05) = 1.3243 CAD/USD

Higher domestic rate → domestic currency depreciates forward
Lower domestic rate → domestic currency appreciates forward
When Canada has LOWER rates than the US, CAD must APPRECIATE in the forward market (F < spot in CAD/USD terms).

4.3 Option Pricing Terminology

TermDefinition
PremiumThe price of the option — what the buyer pays the seller.
Strike PriceThe pre-agreed buy/sell price if exercised.
Intrinsic ValueCall: MAX(Spot−Strike, 0). Put: MAX(Strike−Spot, 0). The immediate exercise value.
Time ValuePremium − Intrinsic Value. The value of waiting. Highest for ATM options.
Time Decay (Theta)Options lose time value as expiry approaches — accelerates near expiry. Hurts buyers, helps sellers.
Intrinsic Value (Call) = MAX(Spot − Strike, 0)
Intrinsic Value (Put) = MAX(Strike − Spot, 0)
Time Value = Premium − Intrinsic Value

Example: Strike $50, Premium $7, Spot $54
Intrinsic = $4 · Time Value = $3

4.4 The Option Greeks

GreekMeasuresKey Facts
Delta (Δ)Price change per $1 move in underlyingCalls: 0 to +1. Puts: −1 to 0. ATM ≈ ±0.5.
Gamma (Γ)Rate of change of deltaHighest for ATM options near expiry. Drives rebalancing cost in delta hedging.
Theta (Θ)Daily time decayUsually negative for buyers. Accelerates near expiry.
Vega (V)Sensitivity to implied volatilityHigher IV → higher premium. Long options have positive vega.
Rho (ρ)Sensitivity to interest ratesLess commonly tested.

4.5 Option Pricing Models

ModelKey Characteristics
Black-ScholesStandard model for European options. Assumes continuous trading and constant volatility. Closed-form analytical solution. Cannot handle early exercise.
BinomialModels price as a tree of up/down steps. Can price American options (tests early exercise at every node). More flexible but iterative. Best for options with vesting schedules or barrier features.
Monte CarloSimulates thousands of price paths. Best for path-dependent exotics (e.g., Asian options, knock-out barriers).

4.7 Corporate Actions Impact on Options

ActionAdjustment
Stock Split (2-for-1)Number of contracts ×2; strike price ÷2. Total value unchanged.
Stock Consolidation (1-for-2)Number of contracts ÷2; strike price ×2. Or: strike doubles, contract size halves to 50 shares.
Stock DividendSimilar adjustment to a stock split.
Cash Dividend (routine)No adjustment to contract terms. Reduces stock price on ex-date, affecting option value but not the contract.

4.8 Put-Call Parity

C − P = S − PV(K)

Synthetic Positions:
Synthetic Long Stock = Long Call + Short Put (same strike & expiry)
Synthetic Long Call = Long Stock + Long Put
Synthetic Short Stock = Short Call + Long Put

4.9 Price Forecasting

ApproachUsesExamples
Fundamental AnalysisEconomic/financial data; intrinsic valueSupply-demand, OPEC quotas, EIA storage reports, earnings
Technical AnalysisPrice charts, volume patternsMoving averages, RSI, Fibonacci, Bollinger Bands, momentum
Blended (Fundamental-Technical)Combines both approachesCOT report (sentiment/fundamental) + momentum signal (technical)
Technical ToolDescription
Moving Average CrossoverShort-period MA crosses above long-period MA = bullish. Below = bearish.
RSI (Relative Strength Index)Below 30 = oversold (potential buy). Above 70 = overbought (potential sell).
Fibonacci RetracementKey levels: 23.6%, 38.2%, 61.8%. Potential support/resistance after a price move.
Bollinger Bands±2 standard deviations from a moving average. Narrow bands = breakout approaching.
COT ReportCommitment-of-Traders: shows net positions of commercial vs speculative traders. Sentiment indicator.

4.10 Tax Implications

  • Professional traders: gains/losses treated as business income (100% taxable, 100% deductible).
  • Non-professionals: gains generally treated as capital gains — 50% inclusion rate in Canada.
  • Professional indicators: high frequency, short holding periods, use of leverage, trading as primary income source.
  • Registered accounts (TFSA, RRSP, RRIF): listed derivatives generally permitted; OTC derivatives generally not.

Element 5: Derivative Trading, Clearing and Settlement

20 Questions · Verb levels: Understand, Apply, Analyze

5.1 Listed vs OTC Derivatives

FeatureListed (Exchange-traded)OTC
ContractsStandardizedCustomized
Counterparty RiskLow — clearinghouse guaranteesHigher — direct bilateral
TransparencyHigh — public pricesLow — negotiated privately
LiquidityGenerally highVariable, often lower
Regulatory ProtectionStrong (CIRO/exchange rules)Less — governed by ISDA/NI 93-101
MarginStandardized exchange marginNegotiated between parties

5.2 Order Types

OrderDescription
Limit OrderExecute only at specified price or better. Price certainty, not execution certainty.
Market OrderExecute immediately at best available price. Execution certainty, not price certainty.
Immediate and Cancel (IAC)Fill as much as possible immediately; cancel remainder. Partial fills allowed.
Fill or Kill (FOK)Must fill entirely and immediately or cancel completely. No partial fills.
On-Stop OrderBecomes a market order once underlying trades at or through a trigger price.
Iceberg OrderLarge order with only a small visible portion — hides full order size to minimize market impact.
Short SaleSelling a derivative you do not own. Must be properly designated.

5.3 Algorithmic Trading

BenefitsRisks
Market discipline and efficiencyLack of oversight
Consistency (no emotion)Technological failure
SpeedOver-optimization to historical data (fails in new regimes)
Unintended consequences (flash crashes)

5.4 Futures Trade Execution

  • Montréal Exchange (MX): Canada's primary listed derivatives exchange. Cleared through CDCC.
  • Introducing broker: handles client relationships and KYC.
  • Carrying broker: holds accounts and clears trades.
Special TransactionDescription
Exchange for Physical (EFP)Simultaneously swap a futures position for the actual physical commodity.
Exchange for Risk (EFR)Swap a futures position for an OTC derivative position.
Block TradeLarge privately negotiated trade reported to the exchange within required deadlines.
Riskless Basis CrossTwo client orders crossed against each other between bid and ask. No directional risk to dealer.

5.5 Options Execution Terms

TermDefinition
ExerciseBuyer notifies broker to buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying at the strike.
AssignmentClearinghouse randomly selects a seller to fulfill an exercised option.
AbandonmentBuyer chooses NOT to exercise — option expires worthless; premium is lost.

5.9A Order Designations

DesignationDescription
Normal Course Issuer Bid (NCIB)Exchange-approved program allowing a company to buy back its own shares. Max 25% of average daily volume per day. Must be designated.
Jitney OrderOrder from one dealer executed through another dealer (introducing → carrying/executing broker).
Short Marking Exempt (SME)Short sales exempt from the normal 'short' designation — applies to qualifying market-making, arbitrage, or hedging strategies.
Program TradeCoordinated basket of multiple securities executed simultaneously. Must be flagged for regulatory monitoring.
Principal OrderThe dealer is trading for its OWN account (proprietary), not a client's.
Insider / Significant ShareholderOrders for insiders (directors, officers) or >10% shareholders must be flagged. Subject to trading restrictions and blackout periods.

5.9B Direct Electronic Access (DEA)

  • Allows clients to send orders directly to the exchange through the dealer's infrastructure.
  • UMIR REQUIRES pre-trade controls to be ACTIVATED before DEA access is granted: order size limits, message rate throttles, price collars.
  • The dealer remains responsible for the client's orders — DEA does not transfer regulatory responsibility.

5.12 Clearing Corporations

ClearinghouseRole
CDCCCanadian Derivatives Clearing Corporation — clears all Montréal Exchange trades. Central counterparty: becomes buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer.
ICE Clear CanadaClears agricultural and energy futures.
OCCUS Options Clearing Corporation — relevant for cross-border trades.

Margin at the Clearinghouse

  • Initial/Original Margin: Deposited when position opened — a performance bond. NOT a loan.
  • Variation Margin: Daily mark-to-market adjustments — gains credited, losses debited.
  • Maintenance Margin: Minimum level. If balance falls below → margin call issued. Must restore to INITIAL margin (not just maintenance).

Guaranty Fund (Default Waterfall)

Sequence when a clearing member defaults: (1) Defaulting member's initial margin → (2) Member's default fund contribution → (3) CDCC's own capital → (4) Mutualized guaranty fund. Client positions can be ported to a solvent member.

5.13A CDS Delivery and Buy-In

  • If a delivering participant fails to deliver securities, the receiving participant may file a buy-in notice.
  • Fractional shares are ineligible under CDS eligibility standards — the delivering participant must pay cash for the fractional portion (e.g., after a stock split).

5.14 Types of Margin

TypeDescription
Exchange MarginMinimum set by the exchange — the regulatory floor.
In-House MarginFirm's own margin — can be higher than exchange margin.
Hedge MarginReduced margin when a derivative is offset by a physical/securities position (documented in Hedge Agreement).
Day Trading MarginReduced margin for positions opened and closed within the same trading session.
Key distinction: Futures margin is a PERFORMANCE BOND (good-faith deposit). Securities margin is a LOAN from the broker. These are fundamentally different.

Element 6: Speculating, Hedging and Other Investment Strategies

26 Questions — LARGEST element · Verb levels: Understand, Analyze (includes calculations)

6.1 Three Main Uses of Derivatives

UseDescription
HedgingREDUCE an existing risk. Like buying insurance. Goal = protection, not profit.
SpeculatingPROFIT from a forecast price move. Accepts risk intentionally.
ArbitrageExploit price differences between related markets. Earn risk-free profit.

6.2 Futures P&L Formulas

Long Futures P&L = (Exit Price − Entry Price) × Contract Size
Short Futures P&L = (Entry Price − Exit Price) × Contract Size
Hedge TypeDescription
Short HedgeOwn the underlying, fear prices will FALL → sell futures. E.g., farmer hedging wheat prices.
Long HedgeNeed to BUY the underlying in future, fear prices will RISE → buy futures. E.g., manufacturer locking in input costs.
Fig 10 — Long Futures Speculation: go long when expecting price to rise
Fig 11 — Short Futures Speculation: go short when expecting price to fall

6.3A Beta Hedging — Equity Portfolios

Contracts to Hedge = (Portfolio Value × Portfolio Beta) ÷ (Index Futures Price × Contract Multiplier)

Example: $20M portfolio, Beta = 1.2, Index futures at 2,000, multiplier $200
= ($20M × 1.2) / (2,000 × $200) = $24M / $400,000 = 60 contracts SHORT

6.5 Futures Spread Strategies

StrategyDescription
Basis SpreadTrade the difference between spot price and futures price.
Inter-Commodity SpreadLong one commodity's future, short a related commodity's future (e.g., crude oil vs heating oil).
Calendar Spread (Intra-Commodity)Long and short futures on the SAME commodity but DIFFERENT expiry months. Lower margin than outright. Expresses a view on the spread between months, not the outright price direction.

6.6 Options — Speculative P&L

Long Call P&L = MAX(Spot − Strike, 0) − Premium
Long Put P&L = MAX(Strike − Spot, 0) − Premium
Short Call P&L = Premium − MAX(Spot − Strike, 0)
Short Put P&L = Premium − MAX(Strike − Spot, 0)

Long Call Breakeven = Strike + Premium
Long Put Breakeven = Strike − Premium
PositionOutlookMax ProfitMax Loss
Long CallBullishUnlimitedPremium paid
Short CallBearish/NeutralPremium receivedUnlimited
Long PutBearishStrike − PremiumPremium paid
Short PutBullish/NeutralPremium receivedStrike − Premium
Fig 12 — Long Call: buy when bullish; loss limited to premium
Fig 13 — Short Call: sell when bearish/neutral; loss unlimited
Fig 14 — Long Put: buy when bearish; loss limited to premium
Fig 15 — Short Put: sell when bullish/neutral; loss if price falls

6.7 Hedging with Options

StrategyStructureEffect
Protective PutLong stock + Long putCreates a floor on downside; upside preserved.
Covered CallLong stock + Short callGenerates premium income; caps upside above strike.
Fig 16 — Protective Put: long stock + long put creates a floor on downside
Fig 17 — Covered Call: long stock + short call generates income, caps upside

Delta Hedging

Contracts to Delta-Hedge Stock = Shares ÷ (Delta × Shares per Contract)
Contracts to Delta-Hedge Options = Portfolio Delta ÷ Option Delta

Delta-neutral portfolio: total delta = 0 → no directional price exposure
Requires continuous rebalancing as delta changes (gamma risk).

6.9 Other Strategies

StrategyStructureProfit condition
Bull Call SpreadBuy lower-strike call + sell higher-strike callModerately bullish. Limits cost AND profit.
Bear Put SpreadBuy higher-strike put + sell lower-strike putModerately bearish. Limits cost AND profit.
Long StraddleBuy ATM call + buy ATM put (same strike)Large move in either direction. Breakevens = Strike ± Total Premium.
Short StraddleSell ATM call + sell ATM put (same strike)Price stays near strike. Max profit = total premium received.
Long StrangleBuy OTM call + buy OTM put (different strikes)Larger move needed than straddle. Cheaper premium. Breakevens = Call Strike + Premium & Put Strike − Premium.
Short StrangleSell OTM call + sell OTM put (different strikes)Wider profit zone than short straddle.
Fig 18 — Bull Call Spread: lower cost than long call; profit capped at high strike
Fig 19 — Bear Put Spread: lower cost than long put; profit capped at low strike
Bull Call Spread:
Net cost (max loss) = Premium paid − Premium received
Max profit = (High Strike − Low Strike) − Net cost
Breakeven = Low Strike + Net cost
Fig 20 — Long Straddle: profit from large move in either direction; max loss at strike
Fig 21 — Short Straddle: profit if price stays near strike; large losses from big moves
Fig 22 — Long Strangle: cheaper than straddle; requires larger move to profit
Fig 23 — Short Strangle: wider profit zone than short straddle

6.9A Weather Derivatives

InstrumentDescription
Cooling Degree Day (CDD)CDD = MAX(0, Avg Temp − 65°F). Measures heat. Buy CDD call if expecting warmer temps.
Heating Degree Day (HDD)HDD = MAX(0, 65°F − Avg Temp). Measures cold. Buy HDD put if expecting milder winter.
Expect warmer summer → BUY CDD call. Expect cooler summer → BUY CDD put. Expect milder winter → BUY HDD put.

Element 7: Integrity in the Derivative Markets

6 Questions · Verb levels: Apply · Governed primarily by UMIR

7.1 UMIR Violations

ViolationDefinition
Best ExecutionDealers must obtain the best possible outcome for clients — price, speed, likelihood of execution.
Abusive TradingTrading that harms market integrity — broad term covering manipulation, deception, misconduct.
Artificial PricingDeliberately trading to push a price to an artificial level to benefit a derivative position. Illegal.
Wash TradingPlacing buy and sell orders to create artificial trading activity without genuine change of beneficial ownership.
Cross-Asset ManipulationTrading in the underlying asset to artificially move the price of a related derivative. Illegal.
Front RunningPlacing your OWN order first, knowing a large client order is coming. Strictly prohibited. Only permissible if client has been fully informed and consented.

7.2 Gatekeeping Responsibilities

  • Know the client's typical activity — understand what is NORMAL for them.
  • Identify suspicious transactions: unusual size, frequency, timing, or counterparties.
  • Escalate immediately to compliance — do not wait for proof.
  • External reporting to CIRO and FINTRAC (anti-money laundering — PCMLTFA).
  • Whistleblower frameworks protect employees who report violations in good faith.
As a registered person you have an ACTIVE duty to watch for and report misconduct — not just avoid it yourself.

Element 8: Standards of Conduct and Conflicts of Interest

6 Questions · Verb levels: Analyze · Closely linked to NI 93-101

8.1 CIRO Standards of Conduct

  • High standards of ethics and conduct at all times.
  • Acting openly and fairly — no hidden agendas or undisclosed conflicts.
  • Acting in accordance with just and equitable principles of trade.
  • Avoiding actions that are unbecoming or detrimental to the public interest.
NI 93-101 ObligationDescription

8.2 Managing Conflicts of Interest

Required sequence (in order):

  1. Identify — recognize that a conflict exists or could exist.
  2. Avoid — structure the situation so the conflict does not arise.
  3. Address — neutralize it (e.g., use a different team, implement controls).
  4. Disclose — if unavoidable, disclose clearly so the client can make an informed decision.
The client's best interests always take priority. Personal gain cannot override client welfare.

8.3 Outside Activities

  • Approval: Many outside activities require prior written approval from the Investment Dealer — BEFORE starting.
  • Disclosure: Approved activities must be disclosed to the dealer and potentially to clients.
  • Prohibition: Certain activities are outright prohibited.

8.4 Personal Financial Dealings with Clients

  • Accepting any consideration (gifts, kickbacks): prohibited.
  • Personal borrowing from clients: generally prohibited.
  • Personal lending to clients: regulatory violation — no exceptions.
  • Taking control or authority over a client's finances for personal benefit: prohibited.

∑ All Formulas — Complete Reference

Every formula from the exam in one place

Futures Pricing

Fair Value (Future) = Spot Price + Cost of Carry
Cost of Carry = Financing + Storage + Insurance − Income Received
Basis = Spot Price − Futures Price (→ 0 at expiry)

Cash-and-Carry (futures overpriced): Buy spot + Sell futures
Reverse Cash-and-Carry (underpriced): Sell spot + Buy futures

Interest Rate Parity

Forward Rate = Spot × (1 + r_domestic) / (1 + r_foreign)
Lower domestic rate → domestic currency appreciates in forward market

Option Intrinsic & Time Value

Intrinsic Value (Call) = MAX(Spot − Strike, 0)
Intrinsic Value (Put) = MAX(Strike − Spot, 0)
Time Value = Premium − Intrinsic Value

Option P&L and Breakevens

Long Call P&L = MAX(Spot − Strike, 0) − Premium Breakeven = Strike + Premium
Long Put P&L = MAX(Strike − Spot, 0) − Premium Breakeven = Strike − Premium
Short Call P&L = Premium − MAX(Spot − Strike, 0)
Short Put P&L = Premium − MAX(Strike − Spot, 0)

Put-Call Parity

C − P = S − PV(K)
Synthetic Long Stock = Long Call + Short Put (same strike & expiry)
Synthetic Short Stock = Short Call + Long Put

Spread Strategies

Bull Call Spread: Net cost = Premium paid − Premium received
Max profit = Spread width − Net cost
Breakeven = Low Strike + Net cost

Long Straddle: Breakevens = Strike ± Total Premium
Long Strangle: Upper BE = Call Strike + Total Premium
Lower BE = Put Strike − Total Premium

Hedging — Contracts to Hedge

Equity Portfolio (Beta hedge):
Contracts = (Portfolio Value × Beta) ÷ (Futures Price × Multiplier)

Bond Portfolio (Duration hedge):
Contracts = (Portfolio Value × Duration) ÷ (Futures Duration × Notional)
SHORT to hedge rising rate risk (bond prices fall)

Delta Hedge (stock vs options):
Contracts = Shares ÷ (Delta × Shares per Contract)

Delta Hedge (options book):
Contracts = Portfolio Delta ÷ Option Delta

Leverage

Leverage Ratio = Notional Contract Value ÷ Capital Required (margin or premium)
Example: $100,000 contract, $5,000 margin → 20:1 leverage

Weather Derivatives

CDD = MAX(0, Avg Daily Temp − 65°F) Cooling Degree Day
HDD = MAX(0, 65°F − Avg Daily Temp) Heating Degree Day

Warmer expected → BUY CDD call
Cooler expected → BUY CDD put
Milder winter → BUY HDD put
